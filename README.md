# POLI 110: Tutorial Materials

Tutorial slides, notes, and exercises written as Graduate Teaching Assistant for **POLI 110, Investigating Politics: An Introduction to Scientific Political Analysis**, Department of Political Science, University of British Columbia, 2024–25 (instructors Michael Weaver and Matthew Wright).

Browse the materials at **<https://aodicheng1219.github.io/POLI110-A.Cheng-TA-material/>**.

## Contents

`slides/` — eight LaTeX Beamer decks used in tutorial: introduction to scientific methods (Week 1), validity and measurement error (6), sampling error (7), counterfactual causality (9), directed acyclic graphs (10), randomization in experiments (11), blocking confounding with experiments (12), and conditioning-based versus design-based approaches to confounding (13).

`notes/` — fifteen written notes distributed to students. Weeks 2 through 12 cover effects of causes, types of claims, the severity principle, concepts and measurement, measuring democratic backsliding, validity and measurement error, sampling error, hypothesis testing, interpreting descriptive evidence, selection bias in the potential-outcomes framework, correlation and causation, and experiments as a solution to confounding. Three supplementary notes cover modernization theory and regime change, necessary and sufficient conditions in comparative research designs, and process tracing.

`exercises/` — a hypothesis-testing exercise with its answer key, and a group activity using a directed acyclic graph to contrast design-based and conditioning-based strategies for confounding.

## Scope and attribution

Everything in this repository was written by Aodi Cheng. It is offered for students and instructors to reuse, with attribution.

Materials that are not mine are deliberately excluded: course readings and textbook chapters, lecture slides belonging to the course instructors, tutorial decks prepared by other teaching assistants, and all examinations, homework, and marking keys. No student records of any kind appear here — no rosters, attendance sheets, grade books, or submitted work.
